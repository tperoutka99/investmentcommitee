**3. Sensitivity**
- **Growth:** Uber's revenue tracks discretionary spending on rides and delivery, so its growth link is consumer, not software. Nominal retail sales are up 6.7% and unemployment is 4.1%, which supports trips and orders. That support is nominal, though: real retail is only about +2.7%.
- **Inflation:**
  - It works both ways. Higher fares and basket sizes lift nominal gross bookings.
  - US auto-insurance and fuel inflation push up rider prices and the cost of driver incentives.
  - A tight labor market makes it more expensive to keep enough drivers on the road.
- **Rates:** The main channel is the discount rate, not financing cost. Uber is priced as a long-duration growth equity, and the 10Y has risen about 100bp in a year to 5.10%. I did not check its balance sheet (that was outside my brief). My understanding is that its debt is mostly fixed-rate notes against a large cash balance, so it is not a meaningful net borrower and higher policy rates hit valuation, not interest expense.
- **Dollar:** A large share of bookings comes from outside the US and is reported in dollars. With UUP up 4.4% on the year, that is a translation headwind for reported growth.

**4. Call: Headwind, Medium confidence.** A 5%+ 10-year, a Fed still hiking and a firmer dollar all hit the multiple and reported international growth, while strong nominal consumer demand only partly offsets them.

## 2. Equity analyst + valuation

**UBER: Equity Analyst Report (as of 2026-11-19)**

**1. Business**
Uber runs a two-sided marketplace across Mobility, Delivery and Freight. It takes a share of $59.8B in quarterly Gross Bookings (GB) as revenue. Earnings depend on trip volume (4.1B trips, +16%), consumers (215M MAPCs, +14%), cross-selling between Mobility and Delivery, and take-rate and ads gains. Autonomous vehicles are the main long-term swing factor: they could bring extra supply onto Uber's network, or let AV owners bypass Uber.

**3. Management**

It will be paid with cash plus new debt, backed by a roughly €14B committed bridge loan, while keeping gross leverage under 2x. The acceptance period ended 11/5/26 and settlement is expected in 2H27. I treat them as value-neutral at cost. Delivery Hero's own debt and EBITDA are not in my data. If Delivery Hero is worth 30% less than the offer price, value falls by about $2.20/share.

**5. Valuation** (damodaran-dcf structure, Python)
- **Inputs:** risk-free rate **5.10%** (10Y, 2026-11-17, TREASURY_YIELD). I assumed the ERP of 4.5% and the industry unlevered beta of 1.15 (relevered to 1.26). Cost of equity is 10.8%, WACC is 10.1% tapering to 8.6%, and terminal growth is 4%. The $10.1B deferred tax asset is used as a tax shield.

- **Growth-invariance test:** with no moat the base holds at $83–88; with a moat it ranges $89–117. About $18 of the base therefore depends on a perpetual moat.

## 3. Short seller (red team)

**UBER short-seller case (as of 2026-11-19)**

**1. Top 3 bear points**
1. **Autonomous vehicles are already cutting Uber out.** Uber confirmed that Waymo will run its own service in Austin and Atlanta from January 2028; the stock fell 9% on the news (web, Simply Wall St, 7/25/26). The 10-Q names Waymo, Tesla and Zoox as current or future competitors (EDGAR). If the best autonomous operator goes direct, the "aggregator moat" fails. That moat carries the terminal value, which is 66–72% of any DCF.
2. **Cash flow is weaker than it looks.** Building up insurance reserves added $2.66B to operating cash flow in 2025, 26% of it (EDGAR 10-K). That build fell 40% year on year in 9M26 ($2.08B to $1.25B, EDGAR 10-Q). Starting from TTM FCF of $10.4B, subtracting SBC ($2.07B) and the reserve build ($1.83B) leaves **$6.5B of owner FCF, a 4.9% yield, not 7.8%.** SBC rose 27% year on year in Q3. Supposedly one-off charges keep coming back: $484M of legal reserves in Q3, a new accrual for Australian payroll tax, and $186M of restructuring.
3. **Drivers are getting scarcer while debt is rising.** Q3 Mobility revenue fell 2% (EDGAR 10-Q). Driver supply is lagging demand in Brazil, India, Canada and the US, and incentives are shifting toward drivers (web, Finviz 11/4). Debt rose from $10.5B to $15.7B in 9M (EDGAR 10-Q), including €4.5B of notes issued 9/15 (EDGAR 8-K). Q3 interest expense rose 36%. Holders have tendered 66.56% of Delivery Hero, so the deal is happening (web, cash.ch 11/10). Uber owes a €700M fee if regulators block it (EDGAR 8-K 7/16).

**2. Weakest assumption:** the base case's **18% long-run GAAP operating margin**, against 12.4% today. The analyst's own reverse DCF shows the current price already discounts 11.5%. A margin of **13–14%** is realistic: autonomous partners will keep more of the economics, driver incentives are rising, the Australian payroll-tax cost recurs, and legal reserves recur. At 14% the analyst's model gives roughly $52–83, not $104.

**4. Where the macro call is wrong:** The strategist says Uber's debt is fixed-rate and it is not a meaningful net borrower. That is wrong. The €4B term loan floats at EURIBOR plus a margin, and €10.2B of bridge commitments remain (EDGAR 8-K 8/7). Pro forma net debt is about $24B. The driver-cost pressure comes mostly from outside the US (fuel in India, immigration in Canada), not from the tight US labor market.

**5. Bear price: $52 (−21%).** First method: $6.5B of owner FCF growing 9% for 5 years, then 3%, discounted at 10.8%. Add $13.1B of investments; subtract $7.7B net debt, $1.3B minority interest, and $4.1B for a 30% overpayment on Delivery Hero. That gives $54. Second method: 12x 2027 EPS of $4.38 gives $52.6.

**6. Thesis strength: 3/5.** The bear case is moderate, not fatal. Insiders bought at $71–76, and the stock already discounts flat margins. Owner FCF at consensus growth comes to about $63.5, which is near the price.

## 4. Data flags (orchestrator sanity check and verification)

**Price check:** fresh FMP quote (2026-11-19) shows price $65.60 and market cap $133.95B. Both match the reports. No flag.

**Conflicts and verification status:**
1. **Floating-rate debt / net borrower.** Macro says Uber's debt is mostly fixed-rate and it is not a meaningful net borrower. The short seller says the €4B term loan floats and pro forma net debt is ~$24B; the equity analyst puts pro forma net debt at $23.7B.
   **Verified (short seller) / Contradicted (macro):** Uber's 8-K of 8/7/26 says term loans bear interest at EURIBOR plus an applicable margin, and the term loan reduced bridge commitments by €4.0B. The Q3 10-Q (9/30/26) shows long-term debt of $15,664M, up from $10,521M at 12/31/25. The $23.7B pro forma figure is the analyst's calculation and was not checked.
2. **Delivery Hero deal status.** Equity says the acceptance period ended 11/5/26 with no result posted. The short seller says 66.56% has been tendered (web, cash.ch 11/10).
   - Deal terms **Verified** (Q3 10-Q): €41.50 per share cash, $14.8B equity value for 100%, minimum acceptance of 50% plus one share including Uber's shares, subject to merger-control and financial-services regulatory approvals.
   - €700M fee **Verified** (8-K 7/16/26): the bidder (Uber) pays Delivery Hero €700M if the agreement terminates because the offer lapsed with certain conditions unmet. Separately, Delivery Hero pays Uber €200M if it backs a competing offer.
   - Acceptance level (66.56%) **Not found:** Uber has made no SEC filing after 11/4/26, so the only source is the web report.
3. **Cash-flow quality.** Equity: FCF yield 7.8% (6.2% after SBC). Short seller: 4.9% owner FCF yield after removing the insurance reserve build.
   **Verified (the inputs):** Q3 10-Q cash-flow statement shows the change in accrued insurance reserves at +$2,079M for 9M25 and +$1,253M for 9M26. Whether to deduct it is a judgment, not a factual conflict.
4. **Waymo going direct in Austin and Atlanta from Jan 2028.** This is the short seller's #1 point, sourced only from the web (Simply Wall St, 7/25/26).
   **Not found** in the Q3 10-Q or in any Uber 8-K since June 2026. The Q3 10-Q does say Waymo runs a commercial fleet on its own platform in addition to the fleet it makes available on Uber's, and names Waymo, Tesla and Zoox as current or future competitors. Not contradicted.
5. **Dilution measure.** Equity: diluted shares −4.7% from the 2024 peak. Short seller: −2.0% from FY23 to Q3'26. Different starting points, not a factual conflict. Not checked.
