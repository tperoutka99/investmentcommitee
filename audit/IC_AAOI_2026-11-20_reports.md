## 2. Equity analyst + valuation

**AAOI equity analyst report (as of 2026-11-20)**

**1. Business**
AAOI makes optical transceivers, lasers and cable-TV (CATV) broadband equipment in Texas, Taiwan and China. It builds its products in-house, starting from the laser chip. In Q3, datacenter was $155.6M (58% of revenue), driven by hyperscaler AI spending on 800G and 1.6T transceivers, and CATV was $110.9M, driven by cable operators upgrading their networks (EDGAR 10-Q, 2026-11-05). Profit depends on filling new capacity at prices that a few large customers will accept. Capacity is about 330k units a month now, with more than 1M targeted by end-2027.

## 3. Short seller (red team)

**AAOI short-seller report (as of 2026-11-20)**

**1. Top 3 bear points (ranked by damage if true)**

1. **The company burns cash and pays for it by selling stock at falling prices.** It spent $1.11B on investment in the first 9 months ($702M equipment plus $407M prepayments), against $613M of revenue. Another $928.8M is committed. Free cash flow was −$779M, against a net loss of −$31M (EDGAR 10-Q 11-05). Five ATM prospectuses were filed in 2026 (424B5s dated 2-26, 3-12, 5-14, 8-21, 11-17). The newest is $550M, filed six weeks after the last $600M program finished at $105. At $39.46 it means about 13.9M new shares (+15%) (EDGAR 8-K/424B5 11-17). The equity analyst left this out.
2. **Revenue quality depends on one distributor.** Digicomm owes $234.5M, 56.7% of receivables (EDGAR 10-Q). That is 2.1 quarters of all CATV revenue ($110.9M). The filing says management "extended longer than typical payment terms to Digicomm in order to ensure Digicomm has adequate inventory on hand." That is the company paying to fill a distributor's shelves, not proof that end customers bought the product. Digicomm was 53.1% of 2025 revenue (EDGAR 10-K). The top 3 customers were 92% of Q3 revenue.
3. **The "first GAAP profit" came from outside the business.** Q3 had an operating loss of −$1.4M. Net income of $6.3M came from an $8.2M government subsidy and $3.1M of interest earned on ATM cash. Without those two items, pre-tax was −$2.6M (EDGAR 10-Q). Non-GAAP gross margin is falling as revenue grows: 31.0%, then 29.8%, then 28.7% (EDGAR 8-K 11-05).

**2. Weakest assumption:** the **1.5 sales-to-capital ratio** (how many dollars of new revenue each dollar invested brings in). The real figure is about 0.5. From Dec 2025 to Sep 2026, invested capital rose about $1.08B (PP&E +$668M, prepayments +$415M, working capital flat). Annualized revenue rose about $542M. Even on the Q4 guidance it is only about 1.0. I re-ran the base model with 0.8: my copy of it moves from $53 to about $31 once the new ATM shares are added. At 0.6 it gives $14–17.

**3. Red flag checklist**
- Insider buying/selling: **Red.** $61.9M of open-market sales by 7 insiders since May 20, and zero purchases (EDGAR Form 4). The CFO sold every month, most recently Nov 10 at $48.83.
- Dilution: **Red.** Shares up 155% in 3 years (35.5M to 90.8M). Counting the ATM, convertible, Amazon warrant and stock awards, about 117M (+29%).
- Receivables vs revenue: **Red.** Receivables grew 85% year over year against 128% for revenue, but customers take 141 days to pay and the Digicomm balance is the concern.
- Inventory vs revenue: **Watch.** Inventory grew 122% against 128% for revenue. It sits 180 days. It is financed by a $347M rise in supplier bills, which the company now takes 235 days to pay. Without that rise, operating cash flow would be −$424M.
- Guidance cuts: **Red.** The end-2026 capacity target fell from about 650k units a month (8-K 8-06) to over 500k (8-K 11-05). The US expansion is delayed by power hookups.
- Downgrades: **Watch.** B. Riley cut to Neutral, citing a "structural headwind" in demand (web). Raymond James cut its target from $178 to $165 (web).
- Negative news trend: **Red.** From about $205 (June Form 4 prices) to $39.46. Reported causes are fears about AI funding (web) and the ATM, which knocked 10% off after hours (web).
- New risk factors: **Clear.** Both 10-Qs say "no material changes."
- Stock comp: **Clear** (2.4% of revenue).

**4. Where the macro call is wrong:** It is right that this is a headwind, but it names the wrong channel. Bank-loan interest hardly matters: about $0.5M per hike, against roughly $1.6B of annualized revenue on the Q4 guide. The real rate channels are (a) the share price AOI sells new stock at, since it depends on ATMs, and (b) hyperscaler access to capital (Broadcom and Intel CEO comments, Oct 12; web).

**5. Bear case price: $20 (range $11–31).** The range has two ends:
- $31: the analyst's base case, with only the sales-to-capital ratio fixed (0.8) and the new ATM shares added.
- $11: also a 12% margin, and return on capital no higher than the cost of capital.

$20 is roughly the middle. As a rough check, tangible book value after the ATM is $27.69 a share (EDGAR 424B5). But that book is mostly plants not yet earning and Digicomm receivables.

**6. Bull thesis strength: 2/5.** "The build is funded" was disproved on Nov 17.
