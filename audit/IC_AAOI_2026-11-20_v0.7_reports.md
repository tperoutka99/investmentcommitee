## 2. Equity analyst + valuation

**AAOI equity analyst report (as of 2026-11-20)**

**1. Business**
AOI makes optical transceivers, mostly 800G and 1.6T parts for AI datacenters (58% of Q3 revenue), plus CATV broadband equipment (41%). It builds its own laser chips and runs factories in Texas, Taiwan and China. Revenue depends on hyperscaler spending and on how fast AOI adds capacity. Customers are very concentrated: the top three were 41%, 28% and 23% of Q3 revenue, and the top ten were 99% (EDGAR 10-Q, 2026-11-05).

## 3. Short seller (red team)

**AAOI short-seller report (as of 2026-11-20)**

**1. Top 3 bear points**
1. **The company runs on outside money.** In the first nine months of 2026, $1.19B of cash went out: operations –$76.7M, capex –$702.1M, equipment prepayments –$406.9M. Trailing free cash flow was –$883M against a net loss of –$33M (EDGAR 10-Q, 2026-11-05). Shares went from 35.5M (Nov 2023) to 90.8M, up 155%. If the $550M ATM fills, that becomes +195%. The last raise sold stock at $105.36; this one sells near $39, so each dollar raised now costs about 2.7x the dilution. The bull claim that "capacity is paid for" is wrong. Getting to 1M units/month by 2027 still needs building, along with a $94.1M cleanroom contract and the Ningbo fit-out (EDGAR 10-Q).
2. **Q3 "profit" came from subsidies, and the cable-TV business is financed by AOI.** Q3 GAAP operating income was –$1.4M. Pretax income of $8.7M rested on $8.2M of government subsidies (Note 14) (EDGAR 10-Q). One distributor, Digicomm, owes $234.5M of the $413.8M in receivables against about $110M of Q3 sales, roughly 196 days. Management "extended longer than typical payment terms to Digicomm… to ensure Digicomm has adequate inventory on hand" (EDGAR 10-Q). In other words, AOI is paying for its distributor's inventory. If Digicomm cuts stock, cable-TV revenue (41% of the total) and that receivable get hit together.
3. **Execution and pricing are slipping.**
   - The end-2026 capacity target was cut from about 650k to 500k units/month (EDGAR 8-K 2026-08-06 vs 8-K 2026-11-05).
   - Q3 non-GAAP gross margin was 28.7%, below the 29–30.5% guide and down from 31.0% a year earlier (EDGAR 8-K).
   - The new hyperscaler deal caps prices and requires AOI to build the capacity (EDGAR 10-Q).
   - A key raw-material contract depends on China granting export approvals (EDGAR 10-Q Note 19).
   - The Q4 guide missed consensus because of a 1.6T parts shortage (web, TIKR). There is no matching 8-K language on consensus.

**2. Weakest assumption**
The 15% long-run R&D-adjusted operating margin. With gross margin near 29% and falling, GAAP operating margin is still below zero, and the price caps limit upside. 10% is the defensible number. The analyst's own figures (13.8% → $39.46, 15% → $43.78) work out to about $3.60 per margin point, so 10% gives roughly **$26**. That is my straight-line extrapolation, not a re-run model.

**3. Red flag checklist**
- Insider selling: **Red.** 23 sales, 0 buys since May 20, about $62.5M sold (CEO $9.8M, CFO $8.3M). Mostly 10b5-1 plans or tax withholding (EDGAR Form 4).
- Dilution: **Red.** +155% in 3 years, with +15% more pending.
- Receivables vs revenue: **Watch.** Receivables +85% vs revenue +127% YoY, but days sales outstanding is 141 overall and about 196 for Digicomm (EDGAR XBRL).
- Inventory vs revenue: **Watch.** +122% vs +127% YoY, now $378M (EDGAR XBRL).
- Guidance cuts: **Red.** Capacity target cut, gross-margin guide missed (EDGAR).
- Downgrades: **Red.** B. Riley cut its target from $92 to $45; Rosenblatt from $220 to $115 (web). Raymond James' $165 target comes from the ATM's own sales agent (EDGAR 8-K 11/17).
- News trend: **Red.** Form 4 sale prices show about $205 in June falling to $48.83 on Nov 10 (EDGAR). Company press releases are neutral (Twelve Data).
- New risk factors: **Watch.** Neither 10-Q added any ("no material changes"), even though the price caps, China approvals and power delays are all new. They appear only in MD&A and the notes (EDGAR).
- Stock-based comp: **Clear.** 2.4% of revenue.
- Institutions: not assessed (no data).

**4. Where the macro call is wrong**
The macro report puts too much weight on debt cost. Q3 interest expense was $1.2M (EDGAR XBRL), so a 25bp hike barely registers. The rate risk that matters runs through equity: AOI has to keep selling stock, so a 10Y above 5% hurts it through the ATM price, not through interest. The report also leaves out China supply risk: 44% of production is there, and a key input needs export approvals.

**5. Bear case price: about $26 (–34%)**
This keeps the analyst's base revenue path ($2.13B in 2027) and uses a 10% margin. If capacity delays also cut growth, the analyst's own bear value of $13.32 is the tail.

**6. Thesis strength: 2/5**
The growth is real (+127% YoY). But the bull case needs margins the company has never earned, and it keeps raising money with cheaper and cheaper stock.
