**3. Sensitivity**
- **Rates (high):** Most of SPCX's value depends on cash flows far in the future: Starship, Starlink at scale, new satellite services. That makes it a long-duration equity, so a 10-year yield above 5% with more hikes priced in pushes its valuation multiple down directly. Debt structure and borrowing exposure: not assessed (no data).
- **Growth (moderate, supportive):** Strong GDP and spending help Starlink's consumer, enterprise, maritime and aviation subscription demand. Government launch and national-security work follows budgets more than the business cycle, and I did not assess it (no data).
- **Inflation (moderate, negative):** Aerospace materials and skilled labour are cost-heavy, and fixed-price government contracts can't be repriced. Subscription pricing can partly pass costs through. The bigger channel is indirect: hot inflation is what keeps the Fed hiking.
- **Dollar (moderate, negative):** A stronger dollar means Starlink revenue earned abroad converts into fewer dollars, and the service gets more expensive for foreign customers. I did not assess how much of revenue is international (no data).

**4. Call: Headwind, medium confidence.** Rising real-economy rates, a Fed set to hike, and a dollar up 6% in three months hurt a high-multiple, long-duration growth stock more than strong GDP helps it.

## 3. Short seller (red team)

**SPCX short-seller report (2026-11-19)**

**1. Top 3 bear points**
1. **Half of revenue is two customers, and the AI customer can leave on 90 days' notice.** Customer B was 34.5% of Q3 revenue, about $5.0B. That is 67% of SI revenue and 52% of the quarter-over-quarter revenue increase. A year earlier it was under 10%. Customer A was another 16.6% (10-Q, 10/22/26, EDGAR). The prospectus (424B4, 6/12/26, EDGAR) says the Anthropic compute deal pays $1.25B a month through May 2029, but either party can end it on 90 days' notice. On the other side, SpaceX has $37.8B of commitments it cannot cancel, $25.6B of them due in 2027 (10-Q), plus a five-year, ~$40B GPU lease (8-K 10/26, EDGAR). Bloomberg reports that Customer B is Anthropic. A rumor on Nov 11 said Anthropic won't extend past mid-2027; Musk disputed it (web only, unconfirmed). If Customer B leaves and costs stay, Q3 operating income goes from +$2.8B to about −$2.2B. (Disclosure: Claude is made by Anthropic. This uses public sources only.)
2. **The profit "inflection" is bought.**
   - Over nine months, free cash flow was −$35.0B against net income of −$0.4B.
   - Operating cash flow includes $4.9B of customer prepayments, and $11.8B of capex is still unpaid in payables.
   - Q3 net income of $4.4B includes $1.0B of other income (of which $561M is an unrealized gain from a related party) and a $390M tax benefit.
   - Adjusted EBITDA of $8.8B leaves out $2.36B of stock compensation, 16% of revenue.
   - Starlink (Connectivity) made 85% of operating income. (10-Q and 8-K, EDGAR)
3. **Share supply and governance.**
   - Lockups: 328–798M shares unlock on Dec 8, with more tranches through 2027 (424B4, EDGAR).
   - Funds selling: Founders Fund held 113M fewer shares in Q3 (−26%) and Millennium cut 89% (web, 13F filings).
   - Control and related parties: Musk controls the company. There is $12.9B of debt from Valor, the firm of director Gracias. A special committee is evaluating a combination with Tesla. (10-Q, EDGAR)

**2. Weakest assumption:** the analyst's sales-to-capital of 0.9 (1.2 in the bull case), meaning each $1 of new capital adds $0.90 of annual revenue. Since Dec-25, annualized revenue rose $38.5B while plant and equipment, acquired intangibles and goodwill rose $88.3B. That is 0.44, or 0.21 without Customer B. It should be about 0.45. At that level the reinvestment needed roughly doubles, and even the analyst's $50 base case sits far below $138.

**4. Where the macro call is wrong:** The direction (headwind) is right, but the emphasis is wrong. Starlink revenue per consumer fell 17% year over year, from $83 to $69 a month (8-K exhibit, EDGAR). Growth is coming from cheaper markets, mostly abroad by that reading. So the strong dollar hurts more than the macro says, and a strong US consumer helps less. The macro also missed that the interest rate on the $40B lease is set at pricing, before the deal closes, so further hikes raise its cost directly. And a 10-year yield below 4.70% would not fix the customer risk.

**5. Bear-case price: about $26 (range $20–35)**, sum of the parts:
- Connectivity: 25x Q3 operating income, annualized ($9.5B), so $237B.
- Space: 6x sales, $27B.
- SI equipment: 0.8x the $89.5B of plant and equipment, $72B, because GPUs would sit idle without Customer B.
- Cursor: half its $42.5B purchase price, $21B.
- Cash: plus $51B net cash, minus the $40B lease.

That totals $367B, divided by 14,112M diluted shares. For comparison, today's price is 199x Connectivity's annualized operating income.

**6. Bull-case strength: 2/5.** Starlink's earnings are real. But the price needs the Customer B revenue to be permanent and capital spending to pay back, and the analyst's own bull case ($110) is below the price.
