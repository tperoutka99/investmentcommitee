**3. Sensitivity**
- **Rates:** Low direct cost. All $15,787M of debt is fixed-rate (0%–5.35%), and nothing floating is drawn. The exposure is refinancing $2,925M due in 2028 ($1,200M + $1,725M) at today's ~5% yields. The undrawn €4.0B term loan and €10.2B bridge float on EURIBOR, which follows the ECB, not the Fed. Euro rates: not assessed (no data). The bigger rate risk is a 10Y above 5% weighing on how the stock is valued.
- **Growth:** Rides and delivery are discretionary spending tied to jobs. With 52.9% of revenue from the US and Canada, strong US GDP, payrolls and retail sales are a clear support.
- **Inflation:** It raises fares and order sizes in dollar terms, but squeezes riders' real income and drivers' costs. Roughly neutral.
- **Dollar:** About 47% of revenue is from outside the US and Canada (EMEA 26.9%). UUP is up 3.0% since Q3 ended. My rough math: that trims about 1.5 points off reported growth. Two offsets: the $5,112M of euro notes shrink in dollar terms, and the €41.50/share Delivery Hero price costs less in dollars.

**4. Call:** **Neutral, Medium confidence.** Strong US consumer demand is offset by a stronger dollar hitting 47% of revenue and a hiking Fed pushing long yields above 5%, while fixed-rate debt keeps interest costs insulated.

## Short seller
**UBER short-seller report (as of 2026-11-20)**

**1. Top 3 bear points**
1. **Robotaxis could cut Uber out of the trip.** The base value depends on a lasting moat: terminal value is 70% of it. Mobility earns 70% of segment profit ($2,362M of $3,386M in Q3). The 10-Q says Waymo runs its own commercial fleet and that "our commercial partnerships could expire" (EDGAR 10-Q, 11/04/26). The FT reported that Waymo told Uber it will enter Austin and Atlanta on its own in January 2028; the Phoenix partnership already ended in June (web, Stocktwits 7/24/26). Uber also sold 168.9M Aurora shares at $6.21–7.10 between June and September (EDGAR Form 4s).
2. **Cash flow is weaker than the 7.8% yield suggests.** TTM free cash flow is $10.42B. Take out stock pay ($2.07B) and the build-up of insurance reserves, which are claims owed but not yet paid ($1.83B), and the yield is 4.9%. Also take out the rise in accrued liabilities, which includes the unpaid Australia payroll-tax accrual, and it is 3.9%. Since 2024, $12.05B of buybacks reduced the share count by only 64M net, about $189 per share retired. The "non-recurring" legal and tax reserves come back every year: $507M in 9M25 and $496M in 9M26 (EDGAR 8-K Ex.99.1, 11/04/26).
3. **Delivery Hero adds deal and leverage risk.** A new risk factor says regulators could require divestitures or other remedies. Uber owes a €700M break fee if approvals fail (EDGAR 8-K 7/16/26). The €14.2B financing pays a floating rate whose margin steps up 90, 180 and 270 days after closing, plus duration fees.

**2. Weakest assumption: the 20% target margin.** On the old revenue basis (adding back $3.2B of UK revenue lost to an accounting change), the 9M GAAP operating margin is 11.6%. That equals the margin the analyst says today's price implies. Q3 alone was 9.8%. The jump in gross margin is mostly that UK change, not better economics. My value: 12–13%, which gives fair value of about $68–75 on the analyst's own grid. Add the analyst's own beta caveat and it is $62.

**4. Where the macro call is wrong**
- **Inflation is not neutral.** Fuel shortages in Brazil and India held driver supply below demand in Q3 (web, Finviz 11/4). The 10-Q says Uber expects driver supply constraints to continue. Strong US demand does not help when drivers are the limit. Q4 incentives are shifting from riders to drivers, which squeezes the share of each fare Uber keeps.
- **"All fixed-rate debt" is only true before the deal.** The Delivery Hero funding floats.

**5. Bear case price: about $53 (-19%)**
- **Multiple method:** TTM GAAP operating profit of $7.03B at 15x (Instacart trades at 21x; I discount for the robotaxi risk) = $105.5B. Subtract net debt including leases ($7.79B). Add equity stakes at $9.7B (book value cut 25% after the $476M Q3 markdown). That is $107.4B, or **$52.6 a share**.
- **Cash method:** $6.52B of cash flow after stock pay and insurance reserves, at a 6% yield, is **$53.2 a share**.
- Subtract another $0.49 a share for each $1B overpaid for Delivery Hero.

**6. Bull thesis strength: 3/5.** The cash flow is real and insiders are buying. But the upside depends on margins and on Waymo staying a partner.
