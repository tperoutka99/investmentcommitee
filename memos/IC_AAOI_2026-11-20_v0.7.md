# IC Memo: AAOI (Applied Optoelectronics, Inc.)
**Date:** 2026-11-20 | **Price:** $39.46 | **Market cap:** $3,582M | **Verdict:** WATCH
**Conviction:** Low | **Position:** None

## Verdict
The base value of $43.78 already counts the full $550M share sale, and it sits only 11% above the price. That cushion is too thin when the bear value is $13.32 and all three bear points are now verified. **BUY trigger:** Q4 results show revenue ≥ $385M, positive GAAP operating income and a Digicomm receivable ≤ $234.5M, with the price ≤ $35 at that point. $35 is about 20% below the base value.

## Thesis
- The growth is real. Q4 revenue guidance of $385–420M is about 3.0x Q4'25's $134M, and TTM revenue is up 77.2% to $747M.
- The price needs a 13.8% R&D-adjusted operating margin. Ciena earns 14% and Lumentum 23%, but AOI's Q3 was 8.9%.
- Pro forma net cash is $227.3M. That is a cushion, but trailing free cash flow is −$883M.

## Macro backdrop
**Headwind, Medium confidence.** The 10Y is at 5.07% and the market prices a December 9 hike at over 90%. The short seller is right that the damage comes through the share sale price, not through interest, since Q3 interest expense was only $1.2M.

## Valuation
At $39.46 the market is pricing in a 13.8% margin on the base revenue path. The analyst's values are bear $13.32, base $43.78 and bull $105.50, so the price sits about 28% of the way up from bear to bull. The terminal value is 87% of the base value. Using the Telecom Equipment beta instead gives $50.80. The short seller's 10% margin gives about $26, but that is a straight-line estimate, not a re-run model.

## Data flags
1. $550M share sale (Verified): I use only the after-sale values.
2. Houston buildings (Verified): $102.25M of cash is gone, which shrinks the cushion.
3. Digicomm (Verified): the concentration is confirmed, so the kill criterion stays.
4. Q3 profit source (Verified): there is no operating profit yet, so the BUY trigger requires one.
5. Capacity cut (Verified): execution risk is real. The prior margin guide is unchecked, so I don't count a "guide miss."
6. Price caps (Verified): they limit upside to margins and back the doubts about a 15% margin.
7. China approvals (Verified): they put a $194.4M supply contract at risk.
8. Auditor change (Verified): prior opinions were clean. Minor, so no weight.
9. Pro forma cash (arithmetic checks; $75.9M not checked): too small to change the decision.
10. Long-run margin (judgment): I treat 10–15% as the range. I ignore the unchecked consensus miss.
